# Thumbnail review: AI Overlord

September 5, 2026. Original candidate: public/images/thumbnail.jpg. The separate public/thumbnail.jpg remains the upstream placeholder; no production thumbnail was replaced.

## Original assessment

The cute cyan robot and traffic chaos are useful. Four neon control cards, title glow, numerous vehicles, and a detailed city compete at small size. The gamepad depicts a robot playing a game rather than operating traffic. Remove tutorial-like information from the promotional tile and demonstrate one action and one consequence.

## New alternatives

- A: public/images/thumbnail-v2-thumbs-up.jpg. Nervous confident robot, safety vest, stop paddle, single comic collision, AI OVERLORD title. Best fit for the initial experience of trying to do the job while making mistakes.
- B: public/images/thumbnail-v3-all-green.jpg. Large cheeky robot pressing a green button, green lights, single collision, no title. Stronger action readability and room for the face at small size; can imply deliberate sabotage. This matches the intended later freedom but should not overpromise the initial prototype.

Both are 512x512 JPG exports under 100 KB. Full-resolution generated masters and 128px inspection copies are in docs/artwork/. Both were inspected at 128px; A's face, gesture, and title remain visible, while B gives the face and traffic-control action more space. Neither is a measured click-through winner. The original artwork and RUN placeholder are preserved.

## Specialists' findings

Art direction favored one worker, an actual work tool, and a single readable disaster. The behavioral review favored A's coping-with-chaos story, while identifying B's more obvious cause and effect. Choose based on the actual playable promise, not attractiveness alone.

Use an original mascot. OpenAI restricts incorporating its logo into independent branding and implying endorsement. Anthropic's consumer terms require permission for use of its brands with other products/services. Neither provides blanket permission for this independent game's cover. These published permissions are not a legal ruling on parody. No real company marks were used in the new art.

## Quick test

Show each candidate on a phone at the same size among the same neighboring tiles. Rotate positions. Ask what the player expects to do, whether the robot is preventing or causing the crash, and which tile they would tap. Use several fresh viewers if possible. Your son's reaction is useful comprehension feedback, not audience-wide conversion evidence. Because A has a title and B does not, equalize title treatment for a subsequent controlled comparison.

## Deployment

Once a candidate is selected, copy its 512px JPG to public/thumbnail.jpg. The candidates are saved for review and are not yet wired as the production tile. Do not imply the generated art is an actual gameplay screenshot; use it to guide the eventual game's visual identity.
